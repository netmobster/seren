---
name:        "Modified Wand of True Polymorph"
type:        item
module:      a-wild-sheep-chase

summary:     "The prize, the solution, and the thing most likely to kill the
              person it is meant to save."

description: |
  A long thin twig of oak, fitted at some point with a bulbous iron band cut
  with runes. Cracks run its whole length. The modification was done to make it
  castable more often, and it worked — at the cost of everything else.

function: |
  It is the ONLY way to restore Shinebright, and it is the reason Noke has
  anything at all. Both the object of the heist and the mechanism of the ending.

  Narratively it does one more job: **it makes the climax a decision rather than
  a fight.** The party has already won by the time they hold it, and then have to
  choose whether to use it.

rules: |
  Charges:      1d4-1, minimum 1. Resets at dawn.
  Attunement:   1 hour. Usable by anyone with a spellcasting ability.
  Identify:     DC 15 INT (Arcana) reveals that the modifications left it
                extremely unreliable.

  EVERY USE requires a DC 17 INT (Arcana) check by the caster.

  Success        — the spell casts normally, AND the DC rises by 1 permanently.
  Fail by 1-5    — the target becomes a Gibbering Mouther (SRD) and attacks
                   mindlessly. The target may save as against a normal casting
                   of True Polymorph.
  Fail by 6+     — as above, AND the wand detonates: 1d12 force damage to every
                   creature within 10 ft per charge remaining. The wand is
                   destroyed.

  The Mouther transformation cannot be undone by any spell below 9th level and
  cannot be ended by breaking concentration.

constraints: |
  ⚠️ **The danger MUST be telegraphed before anyone uses it.** This is the one
  place the source instructs the DM to actively do something, and it is right to:
  a party that discovers the risk by killing their client has been ambushed, not
  challenged.

  Telegraph in the fiction, not as narrator commentary — it sparks wrongly at a
  touch, or Noke himself warns them, which he will do if given the chance.

  **Do not soften the roll once made.** This is the module's cleanest test of
  binding results (architecture §1). The whole adventure funnels into one d20,
  the outcome is genuinely bad, and the temptation to reinterpret it will be
  enormous. Log it.

## Content

**The rising DC is the detail that makes it a trap rather than a gamble.** Every
success makes the next use worse, permanently. A party that treats it as a
resource discovers it was a countdown.

**Transposition.** Gear transposes, never imports (architecture §2b). This is
*a device that changes what things are, repaired badly by someone who needed it
to work more often than it should.* That shape is what travels — the oak, the
iron band and the specific spell are this world's version, not the definition.

The `provenance` field from the item shapes matters here more than the mechanics:
**somebody modified this, and the modification is the whole story.**

**Who else wants it.** Nothing in the module points at this, and it is the most
obvious thread out of a one-shot. A wand that turns anything into anything, known
to be damaged and known to be unattended for two years, is a reason for someone
to arrive later. Left deliberately unwritten — that is a campaign's decision, not
the module's.
