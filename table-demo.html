Seren Table

The thing a solo player doesn't have: a table. Your sheet in front of you, your notes at your elbow, the map in the middle, and everyone's dice where you can see them. Combat state, the party, your character, where the story is, what you actually know, the map, the rules, your notes.

Static mockup. Real characters and real Sheep Chase content, but master-level numbers — debuild() isn't built yet, so a level-5 party doesn't exist to render. The map is schematic; real generator output drops straight in. Tabs are clickable.

Ser'en I lower the staff. Not all the way. I ask him what the collars are for.
Seren He doesn't answer immediately, which is itself an answer. INSIGHT 17 vs DC 15 — he isn't deciding whether to lie. He's deciding whether he's allowed to tell you.

"They can't hold a sword," he says. "Not — it's not cruelty. It's what they've got."
Ser'en Jornis, are you still holding the web?
Seren Barely. He's grey with it. "For about another minute," he says, "and then I'd like someone else to have a plan."

⚠️ Corrected 2026-08-26 — the worse one

An earlier version had a third visible tag, Believed, for facts the player has wrong. That breaks the entire mechanism. If you can see which of your beliefs is flagged, the flag is the answer — you would discount anything wearing it on sight.

Architecture §3 puts true and false both on the DM side. So a player sees Known and Suspected only, and a wrong belief sits in the list looking exactly like a right one. One of the three Known entries above is false. Nothing on this screen will ever tell you which.

🔒 DM layer · 4 facts What is actually true is not yours to read — and neither is which of your beliefs is wrong. On one machine this stays an honour system, the same way not peeking behind a screen does.

Spirit Market schematic

⭐ Two layers, one image. You came in by Ashea Road and you have seen Black Docks, the Upper District and the Old City. Everything else is drawn and unlabelled — the shape of the town is visible from its walls; its names are not.

Mechanically it is a PNG plus a procedural opacity mask, not SVG surgery. One render, one fog layer that grows as you explore — and it works identically for Azgaar at region scale and Watabou at street scale.

District names are the real ones off your Spirit Market export. Geometry is schematic only because 1.6 MB of paths will not inline into a mockup.

SRD 5.2 is CC-BY 4.0 and irrevocable — the one corpus this project may legally reproduce with attribution. It needs fetching and processing into markdown, because a panel cannot browse a PDF and neither can retrieval.

That is the same task blocking the grind probe, which is why it is next.

Scratchpad yours · never read by the DM

Private by construction. The one panel Seren never reads, which is what makes it usable for the thing you are not ready to say out loud.

⭐ A pad per companion, too. If you want to keep a note on what you think Arthryn is avoiding, that is your read — written by you, revisable when you turn out to be wrong. Which is exactly the right place for it, and the opposite of the panel handing you his secrets.

Concept mockup, 2026-08-26. Static — tabs and the map toggle work, nothing else does. Content is real: Ser'en, Jornis, Arthryn and Zephyr from characters/, beats and the front from the Sheep Chase module, conditions verbatim from library/2024/combat.md. Party numbers are master-level because debuild() is unbuilt, so a level-5 version of these characters does not exist to render.

What this is not: an application. Every panel is a view over a file the agent already owns — party.md, scene.md, ledger.jsonl, the module, canon. The DM still reads markdown; this is for the person at the table.
